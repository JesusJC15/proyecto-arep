# Red Flags and Emergency Warning Signs

This document consolidates red flag symptoms across organ systems that warrant immediate professional evaluation or emergency services.

## Immediate Emergency (Call 911/Emergency Services)

**Cardiac**:
- Chest pressure or discomfort with dyspnea, diaphoresis, nausea, arm/jaw/back pain
- Syncope or near-syncope with palpitations
- Severe dyspnea at rest
- Cyanosis (blue discoloration of lips or fingernails)

**Respiratory**:
- Severe dyspnea with tripoding (unable to recline) or stridor
- Hemoptysis (coughing up blood)
- Choking or inability to swallow saliva
- Cyanosis or severe pallor

**Neurologic**:
- Sudden severe headache ("worst headache of life")
- Loss of consciousness or altered mental status
- Sudden facial drooping, arm weakness, or speech difficulty (FAST positive for stroke)
- Sudden severe vision loss or vision changes
- Sudden loss of balance or coordination
- Seizure activity

**Trauma**:
- Severe head trauma with loss of consciousness or altered mental status
- Chest or abdominal trauma with severe pain or difficulty breathing
- Severe bleeding (uncontrolled with direct pressure)
- Open fracture (bone exposed)
- Suspected spinal cord injury (paralysis, loss of sensation)

**Gastrointestinal**:
- Severe abdominal pain with vomiting (unable to keep fluids down)
- Hematemesis (vomiting blood)
- Melena or bright red bleeding per rectum with hemodynamic instability
- Abdominal pain with peritoneal signs (rebound, guarding) and fever

**Psychiatric**:
- Active suicidal or homicidal ideation with plan and intent
- Severe agitation or threatening behavior
- Acute psychosis (command hallucinations, acute delusions)

**Other**:
- Anaphylaxis (tongue/throat swelling, stridor, hypotension, generalized urticaria)
- Sepsis (fever with hypotension, altered mental status, tachycardia, tachypnea)
- Severe allergic reaction with respiratory involvement

## Urgent Evaluation Needed (Within Hours)

**Cardiac**:
- Chest pain or discomfort without clear benign cause
- New-onset palpitations with chest discomfort or dyspnea
- Syncope (any loss of consciousness)

**Respiratory**:
- Moderate dyspnea at rest
- Persistent high fever (>39°C) with respiratory symptoms
- Cough with hemoptysis (blood-tinged sputum)

**Neurologic**:
- New or worst headache in patient's history (without thunderclap features)
- Sudden vision loss or vision changes
- Sudden weakness or numbness
- Confusion or altered mental status
- Recurrent syncope or presyncope

**Infectious**:
- Fever with neck stiffness, headache, photophobia (meningitis concern)
- Fever with petechial or purpuric rash
- Fever >40°C with altered mental status
- Fever with severe headache unrelieved by analgesics
- Immunocompromised patient with fever

**Abdominal**:
- Severe abdominal pain with peritoneal signs
- Acute RLQ pain with fever (appendicitis concern)
- RUQ pain with fever and jaundice
- Severe epigastric pain radiating to back with elevated lipase

**Extremity**:
- Severe swelling with pain out of proportion (compartment syndrome)
- Open fracture
- Severe neurovascular compromise (absent pulse, pale limb, altered sensation)
- Red streaking from wound (lymphangitis)

**Skin/Soft Tissue**:
- Rapid spreading erythema with systemic toxicity
- Signs of necrotizing fasciitis (pain out of proportion, rapid spread, crepitus)
- Large or expanding abscess

**Metabolic**:
- Altered mental status with glucose <70 mg/dL (hypoglycemia) or >300 mg/dL (DKA risk)
- Severe electrolyte abnormality (serum sodium <125 or >150 mEq/L)

## Professional Evaluation Recommended (Within 24–48 Hours)

**Cardiac**:
- Chest discomfort with exertional trigger (not clearly benign)
- New-onset palpitations without concerning features

**Respiratory**:
- Moderate URI symptoms with high fever (>38.5°C) not improving
- Persistent cough with systemic symptoms
- Dyspnea with activity not clearly from anxiety

**Neurologic**:
- Persistent or worsening headache
- Dizziness with focal neurologic features
- Recurrent episodes of presyncope

**Infectious**:
- Fever >39°C that does not respond to antipyretics
- Fever lasting >5–7 days without diagnosis
- Fever in immunocompromised patient without clear source

**Gastrointestinal**:
- Diarrhea >6 stools/day with signs of dehydration
- Abdominal pain with vomiting preventing oral intake >12 hours
- Persistent or worsening abdominal pain

**Urinary**:
- Dysuria with fever and flank pain (pyelonephritis concern)
- Dysuria in male
- Dysuria during pregnancy
- Recurrent UTIs (≥2 in 6 months)

**Musculoskeletal**:
- Inability to bear weight or move limb after trauma
- Severe swelling or pain limiting function
- Suspected fracture

**Psychiatric**:
- Suicidal ideation without immediate threat
- New-onset severe depression or anxiety
- Substance use crisis or withdrawal symptoms

---

## Self-Care with Close Monitoring

**Respiratory**:
- Mild URI symptoms, improving with supportive care
- Mild cough without dyspnea or fever

**Gastrointestinal**:
- Mild gastroenteritis (1–3 stools/day) with able to tolerate fluids
- Mild epigastric discomfort responding to antacids

**Musculoskeletal**:
- Mild strain or sprain responding to RICE
- Localized pain improving with rest

**Skin**:
- Small folliculitis or impetigo in immunocompetent person
- Minor wounds healing appropriately

## Special Populations: Additional Considerations

**Elderly (≥65 years)**:
- Any fever may represent serious infection despite blunted presentation
- Falls or syncope warrant urgent evaluation
- New mental status changes warrant urgent evaluation

**Pregnant Women**:
- Abdominal pain with vaginal bleeding
- Severe headache with hypertension (preeclampsia concern)
- Chest pain or dyspnea
- Recurrent or persistent fever

**Immunocompromised** (HIV CD4 <200, on immunosuppressants, neutropenia):
- Any fever warrants urgent evaluation
- Opportunistic infections may present atypically

**Diabetics**:
- Any sign of infection (fever, wound drainage, erythema) warrants prompt evaluation
- Diabetic ketoacidosis: Severe nausea/vomiting with abdominal pain, altered mental status
- Severe hypoglycemia: Altered mental status, loss of consciousness

---

## Key Principles

1. **When in doubt, escalate** — It is better to err on the side of caution with serious conditions
2. **Pain out of proportion to findings** — Often indicates serious pathology (compartment syndrome, necrotizing fasciitis, acute coronary syndrome)
3. **Sudden onset** — Sudden changes warrant urgent evaluation (sudden headache, sudden weakness, sudden dyspnea)
4. **Rapid progression** — Worsening over hours rather than days suggests serious pathology
5. **Systemic toxicity** — Fever with hypotension, tachycardia, altered mental status indicates sepsis risk
6. **Multiple red flags** — Combination of findings increases urgency (e.g., fever + rash + headache = meningitis until proven otherwise)
